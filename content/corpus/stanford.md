## What the policy covers
"Prohibited Sexual Conduct" — sexual harassment, sexual assault, domestic and dating
violence, stalking, and sexual exploitation. Applies to students, faculty, staff,
postdocs, affiliates, on or off campus (including overseas programs). Retaliation and
intimidation, and violating a University Directive or Court Order, are also prohibited.

Stanford distinguishes **Title IX Sexual Harassment** (severe, pervasive, AND objectively
offensive conduct denying equal access; or employee quid pro quo) from
**Employment-Related** and **University Prohibited Sexual Harassment between Students**
(broader standards for conduct outside Title IX's jurisdiction).

## Consent (California affirmative-consent standard)
**Affirmative Consent** = affirmative, conscious, voluntary agreement to sexual activity;
it must be ongoing and can be revoked anytime. Silence, lack of resistance, or a past/
current dating relationship is NOT consent. It is not a defense that the respondent's
belief in consent arose from their own intoxication/recklessness or failure to take
reasonable steps to check. **Incapacitation** (asleep, unconscious, or impaired such that
the person cannot understand the nature/extent of the activity) means a person cannot
consent; this is not the same as mere legal intoxication.

## Supportive measures
Available with or without filing a complaint, and regardless of jurisdiction.
Individualized; may include confidential counseling (Confidential Support Team for
students; Faculty/Staff HELP Center for others), deadline extensions, course/work-schedule
changes, campus escort, mutual no-contact restrictions, housing/work-location changes,
leaves of absence, and increased campus security. The Title IX Coordinator/Director of
SHARE coordinates these and considers the complainant's wishes.

## Privacy vs. confidentiality (important distinction)
- **Privacy:** information shared only with employees who "need to know." Student records
  protected under FERPA.
- **Confidentiality:** legally privileged relationships (medical/clinical, mental-health,
  counselors, clergy). **Confidential Resources do not report to the SHARE Title IX office**,
  so talking to them will not by itself trigger a University investigation.
- Exceptions exist (e.g., abuse of a minor under 18, or a specific threat to kill/seriously
  harm someone, must be reported). Outside Confidential Resources, the University cannot
  guarantee confidentiality.
- **Responsible Employees** (most faculty, supervisors, RAs/CAs, TAs, advisors, coaches,
  HR, etc.) must report disclosures to the Title IX Coordinator. If you want an
  off-the-record conversation first, seek a Confidential Resource.

## Resolution procedures
Which procedure applies depends on the parties' roles, where the incident occurred, and
severity. Options range from **University Intervention** (addressing conduct without a full
investigation, honoring the complainant's wishes) to **investigation** (impartial, trained
personnel; **preponderance of the evidence** standard) and **disciplinary procedures,
grievances, and appeals**. Details:
https://titleix.stanford.edu/investigationgrievance-administrative-policy-and-procedures

## SHARE Title IX student procedures at a glance
(From a student-made ASSU infographic, "Stanford's SHARE Title IX Student Procedures" —
helpful overview; confirm details with the SHARE office.)

Three procedures; the office decides which applies, so you don't have to know.
- **Title IX Procedure** — respondent is a student, faculty, or staff; incident in a Stanford
  program/activity in the U.S. Investigation + hearing (may include live cross-examination);
  neutral hearing officer decides; free Stanford-provided attorney (about 11 hours + hearing
  time); informal resolution available only if the respondent is a student.
- **SHARE Hearing** — respondent is a student or faculty and the case falls outside Title IX
  (e.g., abroad, off-campus non-Stanford activity, or before Aug 14, 2020). Investigation +
  hearing with written cross-examination; free attorney (~11 hours + hearing); informal
  resolution available.
- **SHARE Investigation** — respondent is staff. Investigation, no hearing; Title IX
  Coordinator decides; free attorney (~4 hours); voluntary informal resolution.

**Title IX process steps:** formal complaint (required for discipline; respondent receives a
notice with both names and details) → investigation (~1 month, then evidence review; you
get ~6 hours with a Stanford-provided attorney and may choose a process support person) →
hearing (usually virtual; live cross-exam by the other party's hearing support person unless
both parties opt for written; ~3 hours of attorney prep plus unlimited time during the
hearing; breaks allowed; you may decline to testify) → decision by the hearing officer
(possible student outcomes include contact restrictions, event/campus restrictions, required
time away, educational classes, community service, expulsion — rare; supportive measures
continue even if not found responsible) → appeal decided by a non-Stanford private judge
(~2 hours of attorney time). **Estimated timeline: 3–4 months** (complaint ~5 days;
investigation & evidence review ~2 months; pre-hearing & hearing ~1 month; decision &
appeals ~1 month). No time limit to file an initial report.

**Alternatives to an investigation:** *Intervention* (non-disciplinary help without the
formal process), *withdrawing the complaint* (any time; the Coordinator may still proceed,
but you don't have to participate), or *informal resolution* (opt in any time after a formal
complaint; you can return to the formal process).

**Amnesty:** you won't get in trouble for drug or alcohol use as long as it didn't put
someone else's health or safety at risk. More: https://share.stanford.edu/title-ix/policies-and-procedures

## Sanctions
Up to and including suspension/expulsion (students) or discharge (employees); respondents
may be removed from programs, placed on administrative leave, or removed from campus
pending a finding.

## External options
In addition to internal reporting, individuals may file with the EEOC, the U.S. Department
of Education Office for Civil Rights (OCR), or the California Civil Rights Department (CRD).
A policy violation can exist even where the conduct does not violate the law.
