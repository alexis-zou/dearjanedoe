## Title IX office / where to report — OPHD
OPHD is UC Berkeley's Title IX (and Title VI) office. It receives reports, provides support,
and resolves matters formally or informally. **OPHD is not a crisis or emergency service.**
Report SVSH to OPHD by:
- **Webform:** https://uctitleix.i-sight.com/portal/Berkeley
- **Email:** ask_ophd@berkeley.edu
- **Phone:** **(510) 643-7985** (711 California Relay: https://caconnect.org/)
- You may also report to the UC Whistleblower Hotline:
  https://secure.ethicspoint.com/domain/media/en/gui/23531/index.html
- Free interpretation/translation is available — email ask_ophd@berkeley.edu (info also
  available in Spanish and Chinese).

**Anyone can report, including anonymously**, to the Title IX Officer, any Responsible
Employee, or offices like Student Affairs or HR (they must forward it to the Title IX
Officer). **There is no time limit for reporting**, though sooner helps.

**Responsible Employees** — any UC employee who is not a Confidential Resource, including
RAs, GSIs/TAs, and other student employees — must tell the Title IX Officer if they learn a
student may have experienced Prohibited Conduct. Exceptions: disclosures at public awareness
events like "Take Back the Night," and in IRB-approved research.

Systemwide Title IX Office: SystemwideTitleIX@ucop.edu, (510) 987-9201.

## Key definitions (UC SVSH Policy)
- **Consent** is affirmative, conscious, voluntary, and revocable. Lack of protest,
  resistance, or silence is not consent; consent must be ongoing. A dating relationship or
  past sex is never by itself consent. A respondent's belief in consent is no defense if it
  came from their own intoxication/recklessness, they didn't take reasonable steps to check,
  or they knew/should have known the person was **incapacitated** (asleep/unconscious; unable
  to understand due to drugs, alcohol, or medication; or unable to communicate).
  Incapacitation is beyond mere drunkenness.
- **Sexual Assault – Penetration / – Contact** (without consent); aggravated if force,
  violence, menace, duress, or deliberately caused incapacitation is involved. Includes "rape"
  and "sexual battery" as defined in the California Penal Code.
- **Relationship Violence** (dating and domestic violence), **Stalking** (sex-based),
  **Sexual Exploitation** (e.g., trafficking, lying about STI/birth-control status, providing
  drugs/alcohol to facilitate misconduct).
- **Sexual Harassment:** quid pro quo, or hostile environment (severe, persistent *or*
  pervasive). Includes harassment based on gender identity/expression, sexual orientation, or
  sex stereotyping (e.g., intentional repeated misgendering or deadnaming).
- **Other prohibited behavior:** invasions of sexual privacy (non-consensual recording or
  sharing of intimate images, sextortion), sex with someone under 18, indecent exposure,
  violating a no-contact order, and **retaliation**.
- Policy covers conduct on UC property, in UC programs (incl. study abroad, research,
  online), and off-campus conduct that has continuing effects on campus; between any genders,
  peers or hierarchical relationships, strangers or not, in person or online.

## Protections
- **Amnesty:** Complainants and witnesses won't be disciplined for student-conduct violations
  (including alcohol/drug use, e.g., underage drinking) around the time of the incident unless
  egregious (e.g., endangering health/safety, academic dishonesty).
- **Retaliation is prohibited** against anyone who reports or participates (or declines to).
- Identities are kept confidential except as required by law/FERPA. Parties may not be
  restricted from discussing the allegations or sharing outcomes.

## Supportive measures & no-contact orders (free)
Available after a report whether or not there's an investigation (Interim Measures during a
process; Mitigating Measures if you're not in one). Examples:
- Counseling, health/mental health, survivor advocacy, housing help, legal assistance,
  disability services, tutoring; fees waived where appropriate.
- Academic: change class sections, advisors, or dissertation committee; extra time; retake
  or withdraw from a class without academic/financial penalty (in some circumstances).
- Work: change of workstation, schedule, location, or unit (voluntary for complainant).
- Changes to living, transportation, dining, work, academic, and extracurricular
  arrangements; **escort services**.
- **No-contact orders:** may be one-way or mutual as an interim measure, but UC will **not**
  bar the complainant from contacting the respondent unless specifically justified (and must
  explain why if mutual). A no-contact order as a final remedy restricts only the person found
  responsible. Respondent restrictions can include moving residence halls, being barred from
  certain events/clubs (incl. Greek life), course changes, or exclusion from campus.

## The process (OPHD / UC SVSH)
1. **Report → Initial Assessment** by the Title IX Officer: health & safety check, immediate
   supportive measures, and outreach explaining your rights and options (including reporting
   to police). OPHD first checks whether the conduct is federal "DOE-Covered Conduct" (which
   requires the federal grievance process).
2. Four pathways:
   - **Closure** after initial assessment (e.g., not covered, not enough information, or your
     request for no investigation can be honored). OPHD may still offer resources and
     measures.
   - **Alternative Resolution** (voluntary; both parties agree in writing; either can end it;
     typically **30–60 business days**). May include separating parties, safety measures,
     counseling referrals, an agreement, education, or **mediation — but never mediation in
     sexual violence cases**. **Not available** when a student is the complainant and an
     employee is the respondent, or when an employee is accused of sexual violence. If the
     respondent breaks the terms, a Formal Investigation can follow.
   - **Formal Investigation** (or the DOE Grievance Process for DOE-Covered Conduct): written
     notice of allegations; interviews and evidence review; **advisor of your choice**
     (including an attorney) at all meetings; status updates every **30 business days**;
     typically **60–90 business days**. The University carries the burden of gathering
     evidence. Standard: **preponderance of the evidence**. If you don't want an
     investigation, OPHD seriously considers that but may proceed to protect the community
     (and will tell you first).
   - **Other Inquiry** — when there's no identifiable individual respondent (e.g., an
     organization or unknown person); typically within 60 days.
3. **Investigation report → outcome.** When the respondent is a student, the finding is
   preliminary — either party can contest it and request a **hearing**. The DOE process
   includes a live hearing with advisor questioning; the non-DOE student hearing does not
   allow direct questioning by advisors.
4. **Discipline** is decided by the responsible unit (e.g., Student Conduct), up to
   dismissal. Both parties are told the outcome, rationale, and appeal rights in writing
   within 5 business days of a disciplinary decision; discipline may be appealed.

- Under California AB 1575, a student respondent can request a University-provided advisor,
  and the complainant then has the same right.
