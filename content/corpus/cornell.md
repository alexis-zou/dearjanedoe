## New York state framework ("Enough is Enough," Education Law Art. 129-B)
New York requires colleges to provide, among other things:
- An **affirmative-consent** standard (knowing, voluntary, mutual agreement; silence or
  lack of resistance is not consent; consent can be withdrawn).
- A **Students' Bill of Rights**, including the right to report to campus police and/or
  local law enforcement (or to decline), the right to confidential disclosure to counselors
  and advocates, the right to supportive measures, and the right to be free from retaliation.
- **Amnesty** for drug/alcohol policy violations for those who report sexual violence in
  good faith.

## Process (follows the federal Title IX baseline; see title-ix-federal.md)
Reporting → initial meeting / supportive measures → (if a formal complaint) notice to
respondent → investigation → review of evidence → hearing with advisor cross-examination →
written determination → appeal. Cornell provides supportive measures with or without a
formal complaint and allows an advisor of choice (attorney or non-attorney).

## Supportive measures & no-contact orders
Academic/housing/schedule adjustments, no-contact directives, and referrals to confidential
support — available regardless of whether a formal complaint is filed. (Confirm the exact
menu and request process with the office.)

## External options
U.S. Department of Education Office for Civil Rights (OCR); New York State Division of Human
Rights; local law enforcement. A civil lawsuit is a separate option with its own deadlines —
consult an attorney.
