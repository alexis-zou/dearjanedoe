**Report vs. Formal Complaint.** A *report* is when the Title IX Coordinator becomes aware of
an incident. A *Formal Complaint* is a written document signed by the complainant (drafted by
COCR with the complainant's input) that starts the resolution process. Cornell has
jurisdiction for a Formal Complaint over current students, faculty, and staff. Even if the
other person is not at Cornell, you're encouraged to report to get connected to support.

**Designated Reporters** must tell the Title IX Coordinator what they know when a student is a
complainant or respondent. Supervisors also have a "duty to respond" to workplace sexual
harassment. Other faculty/staff are encouraged, but not required, to report under 6.4.

**Time limits:** A Formal Complaint against a student can be filed at any time as long as the
respondent is still a student. Reporting sooner helps evidence-gathering.

## Immediate safety / law enforcement
You may report to police, to Cornell, to both, or to neither. COCR can help set up a meeting
with police; COCR will not report to police on your behalf (it may share non-identifying info
with CUPD for Clery timely-warning decisions).
- Emergency: **911**
- Cornell University Police (CUPD): **(607) 255-1111** — https://www.cupolice.cornell.edu/
- Ithaca Police Department (non-emergency): (607) 272-3245
- New York State Police: (607) 347-4440, 1850 Dryden Road, Freeville, NY

Protective orders: Family Court or Criminal Court Orders of Protection; CUPD can issue a

Cornell Victim Advocate can help with Family Court orders; CUPD/Victim Advocate can help with
Criminal Court orders.

## New York Students' Bill of Rights (Education Law Art. 129-B)
You have the right to: (1) report to local law enforcement and/or state police; (2) have
disclosures treated seriously; (3) decide whether to disclose and participate in Cornell's
or the criminal process free from pressure; (4) a fair, impartial process with notice and a
meaningful opportunity to be heard; (5) dignity and respectful health care and counseling;
(6) be free from any suggestion you were at fault or should have acted differently;
(7) describe the incident to as few Cornell representatives as practicable; (8) protection
from retaliation; (9) at least one level of appeal; (10) an adviser of choice at all meetings
and hearings; (11) exercise civil rights and religion without interference.

**Amnesty:** A complainant or bystander who discloses sexual assault, dating/domestic
violence, or stalking in good faith will not face Campus Code action for alcohol/drug
violations at or near the time of the incident.

## Supportive measures
Available whether or not you file a Formal Complaint or go to police; requested from or
initiated by the Title IX Coordinator (titleix@cornell.edu, 500 Day Hall, 607-255-2242).
Examples: help accessing counseling, advocacy, or medical services; academic support and
accommodations; class-schedule changes; work schedule/assignment changes; campus housing
changes; educational conversations; safety escorts; no-contact orders. COCR keeps supportive
measures private, sharing only what is needed to implement them. To challenge a decision on
supportive measures, use the "Review of Decision Regarding Supportive Measures" form
(https://titleix.cornell.edu/forms/).

## No-Contact Orders (NCOs)
- A COCR directive restricting contact between respondent and complainant; can be issued as a
  supportive measure **even with no Formal Complaint**. Non-disciplinary — it does not create a
  disciplinary record.
- Typically **mutual**: no communication directly or through third parties, by any means (in
  person, phone, email, text, social media). You can ask to add a **"stay-away" provision**
  and location-specific terms.
- Available when both parties are Cornell students, staff, or faculty and the conduct may
  violate Policy 6.4. If the other person isn't affiliated with Cornell, consider a court
  Order of Protection (see above).
- **How to get one:** email titleix@cornell.edu to request an intake interview. Effective when
  sent to both parties; a copy goes to CUPD (and possibly HR for employees).
- **Duration:** for the length of a formal investigation (and possibly after); otherwise
  generally **one year** from issuance.
- **Modify / review:** email titleix@cornell.edu or civilrights@cornell.edu.
- **With a stay-away provision**, the respondent must keep out of your immediate area (e.g.,
  leave a dining hall or social event if they can't avoid you; move away on TCAT buses). In a
  shared class, the respondent need not leave — you can request seating or group-project
  arrangements.
- **Violations:** knowingly violating an NCO is itself prohibited conduct; you can file a
  Formal Complaint (sanctions up to suspension/dismissal) or ask COCR to tighten the order. If
  contacted, notify the COCR staff member who issued the order and cc titleix@cornell.edu.

## Advisors (free) & privacy
- Right to a **University-provided advisor** or an **advisor of your choice** (anyone,
  including an attorney), plus a separate **support person**, at all meetings. Advisors can't
  speak for you except to conduct cross-examination at the hearing. If you haven't named an
  advisor at least **10 business days** before a hearing, Cornell provides one free to
  conduct cross-examination for you.
- **Free University-provided advisors (law students):** Complainants' Codes Counselors —
  **complaintadvice@cornell.edu**; Respondents' Codes Counselors — rccoffice@cornell.edu.
- Find an attorney: NYS Bar Association Lawyer Referral & Information Service,
  **1-800-342-3661**; New York Crime Victims Legal Help: https://crimevictimshelpny.org/legalhelp/
- If you're 18+, no university office will contact your parents without your written
  permission.
- If a complainant does not want a Formal Complaint, Cornell honors that unless doing so
  wouldn't adequately mitigate risk to others (e.g., weapon/force, repeat offender, minor
  complainant). If Cornell must proceed, it tells the complainant first.

## Consent under Cornell's procedures (NY affirmative consent)
**Affirmative consent** is a knowing, voluntary, and mutual decision among all participants to
engage in sexual activity, given by words or actions that create clear permission. Silence or
lack of resistance alone is not consent. Consent to one act (or past consent) isn't consent to
another; it can be withdrawn at any time, and activity must stop. A person cannot consent if
under 17, mentally disabled, or **incapacitated** (unconscious, asleep, involuntarily
restrained, physically helpless, or otherwise unable to consent — intoxication may cause
incapacitation depending on its level). Consent is required even if the initiator is
intoxicated.

## The student process (2026 procedures)
1. **Report** → COCR offers **supportive measures** and explains rights and options.
2. COCR assesses whether the report alleges Prohibited Conduct under Policy 6.4; if not, it
   refers you to another office or resource.
3. Then one of these paths:
   - **Informal Resolution (instead of a Formal Complaint)** — only for cases *not* covered
     by the federal Title IX regulations. Voluntary; the Title IX Coordinator meets each party
     separately and proposes terms (which can include sanctions/remedies). Either party can
     withdraw before signing. Once signed it's final, can't be appealed, and you can't later
     file a Formal Complaint on the same allegations.
   - **Formal Complaint** — signed by the complainant (drafted with COCR), or filed by the
     University. Both parties get a written Notice of Formal Complaint, then an
     investigation and hearing, with **Alternate Resolution** available before the hearing.
   - **No Formal Complaint** — support continues; COCR considers whether the University must
     file its own complaint for safety reasons.

**Standard of evidence:** preponderance of the evidence (more likely than not). The respondent
is presumed "not responsible" until a finding.

**Investigation** (generally within **90 business days** of the Notice of Formal Complaint,
extendable for good cause with written notice). Trained investigator; parties interviewed
separately and never in the same room; interviews recorded and transcribed. Submit evidence
and witness requests with the forms at https://titleix.cornell.edu/forms/. Parties get **10
business days** to review the draft investigative record and respond. Participation is
voluntary, but missed deadlines forfeit that step. Prior sexual history and mental-health
records are generally excluded.

**Dismissal.** The Title IX Coordinator may dismiss all or part of a Formal Complaint (e.g., no
jurisdiction, facts wouldn't be a violation, complainant withdraws in writing, respondent no
longer enrolled, evidence can't be gathered, or — viewing the evidence in the light most
favorable to the complainant — not enough to go to a hearing). Both parties get written
notice; a complainant can request review (form at https://titleix.cornell.edu/forms/).

**Hearing.** Private, held over **Zoom**; the parties are never in the same room. A
three-member Hearing Panel plus a non-voting Hearing Chair decide responsibility and
sanctions. Notice of Hearing at least 7 business days ahead when possible. Written opening
statements and witness requests are due within **5 business days** of receiving the final
investigative record. Order: complainant testifies, then respondent, then witnesses;
questioning is by the panel, then each party's **advisor (cross-examination)**. The Chair
rules on relevance before any question is answered; questions about the complainant's sexual
history are generally not allowed. Parties may decline to testify. Written decision generally
within **30 business days** after deliberations.

**Appeal.** Either party may appeal in writing within **5 business days** of the Hearing Panel
decision (grounds include procedural error, conflict of interest, clearly erroneous decision,
unjust sanction, or new evidence). The other party may respond within 5 business days. A
three-member Appeal Panel (Provost, VP for Student and Campus Life, and a hearing-pool member)
decides, typically within **30 business days**. Final.

**Alternate Resolution** (after a Formal Complaint, before a hearing; available in all cases;
voluntary; start by emailing titleix@cornell.edu). Must be completed within **30 business
days** of both parties agreeing to participate; the investigation pauses meanwhile. The
Coordinator meets each party separately and may propose terms, including sanctions or
remedies. Examples from COCR's guide include impact or apology letters, directed study,
reflection papers, counseling or alcohol/drug education with CAPS, an agreed no-contact
order, or facilitated mediation. Once signed it's final, can't be appealed, and the complaint
can't be refiled absent new allegations. If it falls through, the case returns to
investigation/hearing and the panel isn't told about the attempt.

**Sanctions** (if found responsible): oral warning; written reprimand; disciplinary
probation; suspension up to 3 years; dismissal/expulsion; loss of privileges (housing, jobs,
activities); educational steps. **Permanent transcript notation** for suspension, dismissal,
or withdrawing while a Formal Complaint is pending (a suspension notation can be removed on
application at least a year after it ends). Cornell may withhold a degree until the process
is complete.

**Emergency suspension.** After a Formal Complaint, if there's an immediate threat to anyone's
physical health or safety, the respondent can be suspended pending resolution (with a right
to review).

**Retaliation** against anyone who reports or participates is prohibited — report it to
titleix@cornell.edu or (607) 255-2242.

## Policy 6.3 — Consensual Relationships
- Romantic/sexual relationships between **faculty and undergraduates** are prohibited; so are
  **staff–undergraduate** relationships and relationships where one person has (or will
  likely have) authority over the other.
- Someone who has had a relationship with a student may not exercise academic/professional
  authority over them. Certain relationships must be disclosed to COCR and a recusal plan
  created. Students are not required to report.
- Contact COCR (607-255-2242, titleix@cornell.edu); Dean of the Faculty (607) 255-4843.
